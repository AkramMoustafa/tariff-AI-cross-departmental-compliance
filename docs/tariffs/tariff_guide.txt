TARIFF AND TRADE COMPLIANCE GUIDE

=== US SECTION 301 TARIFFS (CHINA) ===
Authority: Trade Act of 1974, Section 301. USTR-administered.

List 1 ($34B): 25% tariff. Industrial machinery, aerospace components, electronics.
List 2 ($16B): 25% tariff. Semiconductors, chemicals, motorcycles.
List 3 ($200B): 7.5%–25% tariff. Consumer goods, furniture, automotive parts.
List 4A ($120B): 7.5% tariff. Apparel, footwear, consumer electronics.
List 4B: Suspended (laptops, phones temporarily excluded).

2024–2025 Section 301 Escalation:
- EVs: 100% (up from 25%)
- Solar panels: 50% (up from 25%)
- Steel/aluminum products: 25%
- Ship-to-shore cranes: 25%
- Medical gloves/syringes: 50%–100%
- Lithium-ion batteries: 25% (rising to 50% by 2026)

IMPACT ON POs: Chinese-origin goods must check HS code against Section 301 schedule.
Total landed cost = customs value × (1 + MFN rate + 301 rate + any antidumping rate).

=== SECTION 232 TARIFFS (STEEL/ALUMINUM) ===
Steel: 25% on all imports; quotas for some countries (South Korea, Brazil, EU).
Aluminum: 10% base; 25% for some countries (China, Russia).
Derivatives: Steel/aluminum derivative products also covered (nails, wire, pipes).
Exemptions: USMCA partners (Canada, Mexico) exempt with TRQ limits.

=== RECIPROCAL TARIFFS (2025) ===
Universal baseline tariff: 10% on all US imports.
Country-specific rates announced but subject to 90-day pause for most countries.
China: 145% total tariff rate (baseline + Section 301 + retaliatory).
EU: 20% proposed rate (paused during negotiations).
Vietnam: 46% proposed (paused).
India: 26% proposed (paused).
Mexico/Canada: USMCA goods exempt; non-USMCA goods subject to 25%.

=== EU TARIFFS ===
Common External Tariff (CET):
- Electronics: 0%–14%
- Automotive: 6.5%–10%
- Textiles/apparel: 6%–12%
- Agricultural products: High tariffs, quotas apply.

EU-China: No FTA. MFN rates apply plus potential antidumping duties.
EU-US: No FTA. Sector-specific tariffs. Steel/aluminum tariffs retaliatory.
EU-UK: TCA (Trade and Cooperation Agreement). Goods: 0% tariff if rules of origin met.

CBAM (Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism):
Applies to: Steel, aluminum, cement, fertilizers, electricity, hydrogen.
Reporting obligation started October 2023. Financial payments from January 2026.
Affects Chinese steel and aluminum imports significantly.

=== ANTIDUMPING AND COUNTERVAILING DUTIES ===
These stack on top of MFN and Section 301 tariffs.

Common on Chinese goods:
- Solar panels: ADD 11.45%–244.69%
- Steel pipes: ADD up to 265%
- Aluminum extrusions: ADD up to 376%
- Hardwood plywood: ADD 183.36%

Check: USITC and Commerce AMCO database for active AD/CVD orders.

=== USMCA (US-MEXICO-CANADA AGREEMENT) ===
Replaces NAFTA since July 2020.
Requirements for 0% tariff:
- Rules of Origin: Must satisfy product-specific RoO (typically 75% North American content).
- Regional Value Content (RVC): Varies by product (50%–75%).
- Automotive: 75% RVC requirement for passenger vehicles.

High-risk area: Transshipment through Mexico to circumvent China tariffs.
CBP actively investigates and penalizes Section 321 abuse.

=== TARIFF IMPACT ON PO RISK ===
HIGH TARIFF RISK indicators:
- Origin: China
- HS codes in Section 301 lists 1–4
- Steel/aluminum content
- Electronics, semiconductors, solar

MITIGATION OPTIONS:
1. Country of origin change: Manufacturing in Vietnam, India, Mexico
2. First Sale Valuation: Use factory price rather than middleman price
3. Tariff engineering: Modify product to change HS classification
4. Foreign Trade Zones (FTZ): Defer/avoid duty on re-exported goods
5. Chapter 98 provisions: US goods returned, duty-free on US-content portion
6. Bonded Warehouse: Delay payment; useful if tariff rates expected to change

=== HS CODE CLASSIFICATION ===
HS codes determine applicable tariff rate.
First 6 digits: International standard (World Customs Organization)
7–10 digits: Country-specific (HTS for US, Combined Nomenclature for EU)

Misclassification risk: Customs penalties up to 4× underpaid duties.
Binding Ruling: Request from CBP or HMRC for certainty before importing.

=== LANDED COST FORMULA ===
Landed Cost = Product Cost + Freight + Insurance + Customs Duty + Harbor Fees + Broker Fees
Customs Duty = Customs Value × (MFN Rate + AD/CVD Rate + Section 301 Rate)
Customs Value (US) = Transaction value (FOB price + packing)
Customs Value (EU) = CIF value (Cost + Insurance + Freight to EU border)
