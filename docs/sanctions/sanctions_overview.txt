SANCTIONS AND TRADE COMPLIANCE GUIDE

=== US SANCTIONS PROGRAMS ===
Administered by: OFAC (Office of Foreign Assets Control), US Treasury

Key Lists:
1. SDN List (Specially Designated Nationals): Hard block. US persons cannot transact.
2. Consolidated Sanctions List: Comprehensive blocked parties.
3. BIS Entity List: Export license required; often effectively prohibited.
4. BIS Denied Persons List: Complete denial of export privileges.
5. OFAC Non-SDN Lists: Sectoral sanctions; partial restrictions.

Country Programs (Comprehensive Embargo):
- Iran: Near-total embargo. Oil, financial transactions, arms prohibited. OFAC ITSR.
- Cuba: Embargo since 1962. Limited exceptions for humanitarian goods.
- North Korea: Comprehensive. UN Security Council resolutions reinforce.
- Russia (2022+): Broad sectoral sanctions. Energy, financial, defense sectors.
- Syria: Comprehensive embargo.

Sectoral Sanctions (Russia):
- Finance: Major Russian banks sanctioned (Sberbank, VTB, Gazprombank).
- Energy: Prohibition on new investment in Russian energy sector.
- Defense: Prohibition on goods/services to Russian defense sector.
- Luxury goods: Prohibition on export of luxury items to Russia.

Secondary Sanctions Risk:
- Non-US companies can face sanctions for doing business with sanctioned entities.
- "SDN Plus 50": Entities 50%+ owned by SDN are also effectively sanctioned.

=== EU SANCTIONS ===
Administered by: European Council, implemented by member states.

Russia Sanctions (14 packages as of 2024):
- Asset freezes on Russian oligarchs, officials.
- Import bans: Russian oil cap ($60/barrel), coal, steel, luxury goods.
- Export bans: Dual-use goods, aviation, advanced technology.
- SWIFT exclusion: Major Russian banks cut from SWIFT.
- Trade value restrictions: No transactions over €100K with listed entities.

Iran: Arms embargo, nuclear-related sanctions aligned with US.
Belarus: Sanctions on regime officials and sectors supporting Russia.

=== UK SANCTIONS (POST-BREXIT) ===
UK maintains own autonomous sanctions regime via OFSI.
Closely mirrors EU/US but with UK-specific additions.
UK Global Anti-Corruption Sanctions targeting kleptocracy.

=== SUPPLY CHAIN COMPLIANCE ===

UFLPA (Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act) - Effective June 2022:
- Rebuttable presumption: All goods from Xinjiang, China presumed made with forced labor.
- Applies to: Cotton, polysilicon (solar panels), tomatoes, and derivatives.
- Enforcement: CBP holds shipments. Importer must prove no forced labor link.
- High-risk suppliers: Check UFLPA Entity List.
- Due diligence: Map supply chain to sub-tier suppliers in Xinjiang.

Conflict Minerals (Section 1502, Dodd-Frank):
- Applies to: Tin, tantalum, tungsten, gold (3TG) from DRC conflict zones.
- Requirement: Annual disclosure to SEC for public companies.
- RCOI (Reasonable Country of Origin Inquiry) required.

Child Labor / Forced Labor (TVPA):
- CBP Withhold Release Orders (WRO): Block goods from specific suppliers.
- Key WROs: Malaysian glove manufacturers, Chinese fishing vessels, Brazilian beef.

=== SANCTIONS SCREENING REQUIREMENTS ===

When to screen:
- All new supplier relationships.
- All PO counterparties (buyer, seller, intermediaries).
- Banks and financial institutions involved in transaction.
- Freight forwarders, customs brokers, carriers.
- Any party receiving >5% of transaction value.

Screening frequency:
- Onboarding: Full screen before first transaction.
- Ongoing: Minimum annual rescreening; real-time for high-risk.
- Trigger events: Ownership changes, news alerts, geography changes.

Red flags requiring enhanced due diligence:
- Payment via high-risk jurisdiction (BVI, Cayman, Panama, UAE).
- Unusual payment terms (advance payment, non-standard amounts).
- Reluctance to disclose beneficial ownership.
- Goods inconsistent with buyer's stated business.
- Routing through multiple intermediaries with no apparent business purpose.
- Price significantly below market (may indicate sanctions evasion).

=== GEOPOLITICAL RISK FACTORS ===

China-Taiwan:
- Military tensions increasing. Insurance premiums rising for Taiwan Strait transits.
- Scenario planning: Conflict would disrupt 50%+ of global semiconductor supply.
- TSMC, ASML, Foxconn supply chain disruption risk.
- Mitigation: Dual-sourcing, safety stock, reshoring/nearshoring.

Russia-Ukraine:
- Neon gas (used in chip manufacturing): 90% sourced from Ukraine pre-war.
- Wheat, sunflower oil: Black Sea grain corridor disrupted periodically.
- Energy: European manufacturers face higher energy costs.

Middle East (Iran/Houthis):
- Red Sea attacks by Houthi forces: Significant freight cost increase (2024+).
- Gulf of Oman: Iranian harassment of shipping. Dual-use goods at risk.
- Israel-Gaza: Regional instability affecting Aqaba port, Israeli exports.

=== RISK SCORING FOR POs ===
HIGH GEOPOLITICAL RISK:
- Origin/destination: Russia, Iran, North Korea, Myanmar, Belarus, Cuba
- Transit through: Strait of Hormuz, Red Sea/Gulf of Aden, Taiwan Strait
- Counterparty: Entity on any denied party list

MEDIUM GEOPOLITICAL RISK:
- Origin: China (tariff risk, UFLPA risk for Xinjiang-linked goods)
- Transit through: Suez Canal, South China Sea
- Counterparty: Entities in sanctioned sectors (Russian energy, Iranian banking)

LOW GEOPOLITICAL RISK:
- Origin: USMCA countries, EU members, Australia, Japan, South Korea
- Transit: Atlantic, stable Pacific routes
- Counterparty: Well-established multinational with clean compliance history

=== MITIGATION RECOMMENDATIONS ===
1. Denied Party Screening: Automated real-time screening of all counterparties.
2. Country of origin documentation: Certificates of origin, preferential origin declarations.
3. Supply chain mapping: Tier 1, 2, 3 supplier identification.
4. Trade finance: Letters of credit reduce counterparty risk.
5. Trade credit insurance: Protect against buyer default, political risk.
6. Bonding and customs guarantees: Reduce customs duty exposure.
7. Legal review: Export control classification (EAR99, ECCN) for dual-use goods.
8. Freight insurance: War risk, political risk riders for high-risk routes.
