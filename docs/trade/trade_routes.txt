GLOBAL TRADE ROUTES AND RISK GUIDE

=== CHINA TO EUROPE (CN → EU) ===
Primary Routes:
- Suez Canal route: 20–25 days transit. Most common. High traffic, moderate piracy risk in Red Sea.
- Trans-Siberian Rail: 14–18 days. Higher cost. Disrupted by Russia-Ukraine conflict sanctions.
- Cape of Good Hope: 30–35 days. Avoids Suez surcharges. Used during canal disruptions.

Key Risks on China–EU Routes:
- Red Sea/Houthi attacks (2024–2025): Significant disruption. 30–40% increase in shipping costs.
  Many carriers rerouting via Cape of Good Hope, adding 10–14 days and $1,000–$3,000 per TEU.
- Port congestion: Rotterdam, Hamburg, Felixstowe frequently congested.
- China export controls on critical minerals and dual-use goods.
- EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) affecting imports of steel, aluminum, cement, fertilizers.
- Sanctions on Russian intermediaries disrupting overland routes.

=== CHINA TO USA (CN → US) ===
Transpacific Routes:
- West Coast (LA/Long Beach): 14–18 days. High volume. Port congestion risk.
- East Coast (New York, Savannah): 28–32 days via Panama Canal.

Key Risks:
- Section 301 tariffs: 25% on $360B+ of Chinese goods; up to 100% on EVs, solar panels, medical devices.
- Section 232 steel/aluminum tariffs: 25% steel, 10% aluminum.
- Xinjiang import restrictions (UFLPA): Presumptive ban on goods from Xinjiang.
- Panama Canal drought restrictions: Reduced vessel transits since 2023, added delays.

=== INCOTERMS RISK ALLOCATION ===
- EXW: Maximum risk on buyer. Seller not responsible beyond factory gate.
- FOB: Risk transfers at origin port. Buyer arranges freight and insurance.
- CIF: Seller provides cost, insurance, freight. Buyer takes risk at destination port.
- DDP: Maximum seller responsibility. Seller handles all duties, taxes, delivery.

=== PORT RISK RATINGS ===
HIGH RISK PORTS:
- Aden (Yemen): Active conflict zone. Avoid.
- Bandar Abbas (Iran): Sanctions risk. US persons prohibited.
- Vladivostok (Russia): Sanctioned. EU and US carriers restricted.

MEDIUM RISK PORTS:
- Shanghai/Ningbo: Periodic congestion, COVID-era disruption history.
- Colombo (Sri Lanka): Transit hub, financial instability risk.

LOW RISK PORTS:
- Rotterdam (Netherlands): World-class infrastructure.
- Singapore: Major transshipment hub. Low corruption.
- Los Angeles/Long Beach: Largest US import gateway.

=== DELAY RISK FACTORS ===
- Seasonal typhoons: July–November, South China Sea/Western Pacific. 2–5 day delays.
- Lunar New Year: January–February. Chinese factories close 2–4 weeks. Pre-holiday surge.
- Geopolitical escalation: Taiwan Strait tensions increase insurance premiums by 10–25%.
- Labor strikes: US West Coast ILWU, European port strikes historically cause 5–30 day delays.

=== ROUTE RISK SCORING ===
China → Germany: HIGH RISK (Red Sea rerouting, EU sanctions complexity, CBAM)
China → USA: HIGH RISK (Section 301 tariffs, UFLPA, transpacific congestion)
Mexico → USA: LOW RISK (USMCA preferential access, short transit times)
Vietnam → USA: MEDIUM RISK (Section 232 circumvention scrutiny, GSP eligibility questions)
India → EU: LOW-MEDIUM RISK (EU-India FTA negotiations, stable route via Suez or Oman)
